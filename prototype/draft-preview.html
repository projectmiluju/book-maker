The Silent Architecture of Memory

A collection of notes from the edge of a forgotten summer.

Chapter One

It began with the light. Not midday, but the specific, honeyed kind that arrives between August and September and makes every room feel briefly forgivable.

The house kept a silence thick enough to lean against. Even the dust seemed to move with intention, as though the air had been rehearsing this scene without me.

Section Two

Writing there felt less like invention and more like excavation. The words were already present in the walls. I only needed to sit long enough to hear them.

To write is to return to small things before they vanish: the scrape of a chair, the narrow blue of evening, the way a voice stays in a room after the body leaves.
