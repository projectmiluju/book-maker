# Book Maker Success Metrics

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the Book Maker MVP should be evaluated.

The goal is not to measure publishing success yet.

The goal is to verify whether users actually move through the core product loop:

- write short entries
- accumulate them over time
- revisit them
- group them into a draft
- feel motivated to continue

## 2. Core Product Hypothesis

If users are given a low-pressure writing tool and a clear archive-to-draft path, they will be more likely to keep writing and will begin to see their writing as material for a future book.

## 3. Primary Success Signals

The MVP should prove the following:

1. users can start writing quickly
2. users return to past writing
3. users can create a draft without confusion
4. the draft preview creates motivation to continue

## 4. North Star For MVP

The most important MVP success event is:

A user creates multiple entries and turns them into a draft preview.

This is the clearest sign that the product is delivering its core value.

## 5. Key Metrics

### 5.1 Activation

Definition:

- percentage of new users who create their first entry

Why it matters:

- if users do not write immediately, the product fails at its first promise

Suggested target for early testing:

- strong signal if most test users create at least one entry in their first session

### 5.2 Early Retention Behavior

Definition:

- percentage of users who create at least 3 entries within their first 7 days

Why it matters:

- the product depends on accumulation, not one-time writing

Suggested target for early testing:

- users should naturally add multiple entries without needing heavy prompting

### 5.3 Archive Revisit Rate

Definition:

- percentage of users who open previously written entries after creating them

Why it matters:

- the archive must be useful, not just passive storage

Suggested signal:

- users should revisit old writing to reread, edit, or select for a draft

### 5.4 Draft Creation Rate

Definition:

- percentage of users with multiple entries who create at least one draft

Why it matters:

- this measures whether the product successfully bridges archive and book behavior

Suggested signal:

- users who have enough writing should be able to form a draft without friction

### 5.5 Draft Preview Rate

Definition:

- percentage of draft creators who open the preview screen

Why it matters:

- the preview is the product's emotional proof point

Suggested signal:

- users should want to see their writing in book-like form

### 5.6 Post-Preview Writing Rate

Definition:

- percentage of users who write another entry after viewing a draft preview

Why it matters:

- the preview should increase motivation, not end the journey

Suggested signal:

- a meaningful portion of users should continue writing after seeing their draft

## 6. Qualitative Success Criteria

Quantitative metrics alone are not enough in the MVP stage.

The following user reactions are strong qualitative signals:

- "This feels easier than trying to write a book from scratch."
- "I can finally see what I have already written."
- "My old writing does not feel lost anymore."
- "This makes me want to keep writing."
- "I did not realize these pieces could already belong together."

## 7. Failure Signals

The MVP should be reconsidered if users frequently show these behaviors:

- they hesitate too long before writing the first entry
- they write one entry and never return
- they do not revisit old entries
- they do not understand why drafts exist
- the draft preview feels like just another list instead of a book beginning

## 8. Metrics By Product Stage

### Stage A. Internal Prototype Testing

Focus on:

- first entry creation
- clarity of archive
- clarity of draft creation
- emotional response to preview

### Stage B. Small User Test

Focus on:

- repeat entry creation within one week
- first-time draft creation
- interview feedback about product motivation

### Stage C. Early MVP Release

Focus on:

- activation rate
- 7-day repeat writing behavior
- draft creation rate
- post-preview writing behavior

## 9. Measurement Principles

- prioritize simple behavioral tracking over complex analytics
- pair product metrics with short user interviews
- treat motivation as a measurable product outcome
- avoid vanity metrics that do not reflect writing behavior
