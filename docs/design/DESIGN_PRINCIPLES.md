# Book Maker Design Principles

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the design principles for Book Maker.

Its purpose is to guide:

- internal design decisions
- external AI-generated design exploration
- consistency across landing pages and app screens

This is not a visual style sheet.

It is a product design standard.

## 2. Product Design Goal

Book Maker should help users feel that their short writing is meaningful, accumulative, and capable of becoming a book.

The interface must reduce pressure, protect emotional focus, and make writing feel owned rather than disposable.

## 3. Core Emotional Direction

The product should feel:

- quiet
- intimate
- literary
- warm
- spacious
- reflective

It should not feel:

- noisy
- dashboard-heavy
- overly technical
- generic productivity software
- like a social feed

## 4. Primary UX Principles

### 4.1 Writing Must Feel Easy To Begin

The interface should reduce the fear of starting.

Implications:

- avoid heavy setup before writing
- avoid overwhelming controls
- let the user focus on text quickly

### 4.2 The Writing Itself Must Be The Hero

The UI must support writing, not compete with it.

Implications:

- typography matters more than decorative chrome
- the body text should visually lead the screen
- controls should stay secondary

### 4.3 Accumulation Must Feel Meaningful

Saved writing should feel like part of a growing body of work.

Implications:

- archive views should feel curated, not disposable
- timestamps and structure should reinforce continuity
- the user should sense progress without feeling productivity pressure

### 4.4 Drafting Must Feel Like Becoming A Book

The move from archive to draft should feel significant.

Implications:

- draft views should feel more structured than archive views
- preview should feel closer to reading than editing
- the transition should create emotional reward

## 5. Visual Direction

### 5.1 Typography First

Typography should carry much of the product's identity.

Implications:

- prioritize readable, character-rich type choices
- create clear hierarchy between title, body, metadata, and navigation
- preserve generous line height and spacing

### 5.2 Whitespace Is Functional

Whitespace is not decoration.

It creates:

- calm
- readability
- a book-like atmosphere
- emotional breathing room

### 5.3 Avoid Feed Aesthetics

The archive must not look like a social timeline.

Implications:

- avoid loud cards, badges, and engagement-like UI
- avoid visual patterns associated with likes, feeds, and social content ranking

### 5.4 Avoid Admin Tool Aesthetics

The app must not look like enterprise software.

Implications:

- avoid dense control bars
- avoid table-heavy layouts for core writing screens
- avoid exposing too many secondary actions at once

## 6. Interaction Principles

### 6.1 Saving Should Be Reassuring, Not Distracting

Users need confidence that their writing is safe.

Implications:

- show clear but quiet autosave feedback
- avoid disruptive modals for routine saving
- handle failure states clearly

### 6.2 Motion Should Support Meaning

Animation should reinforce emotional transitions.

Implications:

- use subtle transitions when moving from archive to draft
- use restrained motion in reading views
- do not add motion that feels decorative without purpose

### 6.3 Reading Mode Should Feel Different From Editing Mode

The preview experience should have a different tone from the editor.

Implications:

- reduce editing affordances in preview
- increase reading comfort and focus
- make the user feel they are seeing a manuscript, not a form

## 7. Landing Page Principles

The landing page should:

- communicate the product story clearly
- emotionally resonate with users who have postponed writing
- show that short writing can become a book

The landing page should not:

- look like a startup template
- over-explain technical features
- feel louder than the product itself

## 8. App Design Principles

The private app should:

- feel focused
- feel trustworthy
- feel personal
- support long-term use

The app should not:

- imitate social media
- overwhelm the user with options
- make the user feel like they need to become a professional writer immediately

## 9. Mobile Principles

The MVP is web-first, but mobile responsiveness is mandatory.

Implications:

- writing must remain comfortable on small screens
- touch targets must be usable
- reading width and spacing must remain legible
- navigation should stay simple and stable

## 10. Future Public Reading Principles

When public reading arrives later, it should feel like reading a digital book, not browsing a post feed.

Implications:

- page transitions should feel intentional
- typography and spacing should support immersion
- reader controls should remain restrained

## 11. AI Design Tool Usage Rule

External AI-generated design should be evaluated by these principles, not by visual novelty alone.

A design is acceptable only if it:

- supports writing focus
- preserves emotional calm
- distinguishes archive from draft
- feels book-oriented rather than feed-oriented
- works on both desktop and mobile
